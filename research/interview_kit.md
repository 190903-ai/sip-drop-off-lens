# User Interview Kit: First-Time SIP Continuation

**Project:** Groww Growth, NextLeap PM Fellowship (Part 3)
**Goal metric:** % of first-time MF investors with an active SIP (at least 1 successful instalment in the last 30 days) 12 months after their first SIP.
**Research question:** Why does SIP continuation break down for first-time investors who *meant* to keep going?
**Status:** Target segment not final. Core guide works for all first-time SIP investors. Swap in 1-2 optional modules (Section 5) once the discovery engine picks the segment.

> Framing guardrail: "investors stop when markets fall" is NOT an acceptable finding. If a participant says it, treat it as the start of the conversation, not the answer. Ask what *exactly* they saw, where, what they thought it meant, what else was going on that month, and who they talked to.

## 1. Methodology rationale

**Format.** Semi-structured 1:1 interviews, 30-40 min, video call or phone. One interviewer; a second person takes notes if possible. Record only with permission.

**Why semi-structured 1:1.** Continuation is a private money decision. People rarely admit in a group that they paused a Rs 500 SIP because rent was due. A 1:1 conversation with a fixed spine (the 6 brief areas) and freedom to follow surprises gives comparable data across 5-6 people while still catching reasons we have not guessed.

**Why a timeline / critical-incident technique.** People are poor at explaining *why* they behave a certain way in general, but good at recalling specific moments. Asking them to walk month by month from "the day you started" to today, then zooming into concrete incidents (the first red number, the first failed autopay, the month they skipped), gives us real behaviour and context instead of opinions. It also exposes the gap between intent ("I wanted to invest for 5 years") and what actually happened.

**Why mix continuers AND pausers/stoppers.** The drop-off contrast is the whole point. If we only talk to people who stopped, we cannot tell which experiences are specific to stopping and which everyone goes through. Continuers show what kept them going despite the same bad months (a goal, a person, autopay they forgot about, not checking). Target at least 2 of each.

**Avoiding friends-and-family bias.**
- Prefer second-degree contacts (a friend's colleague, a college junior) over close friends.
- Do not tell them the hypothesis or that you work on Growth. Say "I'm studying how people experience their first SIP."
- Make it explicitly safe to report stopping: "There are no right answers. Lots of people pause or stop; that's exactly what we want to learn from."
- Do not interview anyone whose investing you influenced.
- Ask about past behaviour, not opinions of Groww, so politeness has less room to distort answers.
- Note the closeness of the relationship on each sheet (close / acquaintance / second-degree) and check in synthesis whether close contacts sound more positive.

**Limitations of n = 5-6.**
- Not statistically representative. We can find *reasons* and *mechanisms*, not *how many* people have them.
- Sample skews urban, educated, salaried, 20-35, and friendly to the interviewer.
- Recall bias: people rationalise past decisions; stoppers may over-blame external events.
- Use interviews to validate, challenge or add to the discovery engine's themes (which have volume), and to explain the "why" behind them. Never quote interview counts as percentages in the deck ("3 of 6" is fine; "50% of users" is not).

## 3. Consent script

Read at the start, before recording. Get a clear verbal "yes" to each bolded item.

> "Thanks for making time. Before we start, a few quick things.
>
> I'm doing a research project on how people experience their first SIP: why they started, what's happened since, and what makes it easy or hard to keep going. There are no right or wrong answers. Lots of people pause or stop, and that's just as useful to hear about as people who keep going.
>
> **This is research, not financial advice.** I won't tell you what to do with your investments, and I'm not selling anything.
>
> **I will not ask for your account numbers, PAN, folio numbers, or screenshots of your portfolio.** Please don't share them. If you want to mention an amount, a rough range is fine, and you can skip anything you're not comfortable with.
>
> **Your name won't be used.** You'll be referred to as a code like 'P3'. I may quote things you say, but without your name or any detail that identifies you.
>
> **Is it okay if I record this call?** It's only so I don't miss anything in my notes. Only I [and my project reviewer] will hear it, and I'll delete it after the project.
>
> You can skip any question or stop at any time. It'll take about 30-40 minutes.
>
> **Are you okay to go ahead?**"

## 4. Discussion guide (35-40 min)

**Interviewer rules**
- Ask about **the past**, not the future: "Tell me about the last time you..." / "What happened next?" / "Walk me through..."
- Avoid "Would you...?", "Do you like...?", "Don't you think...?"
- Never suggest a reason ("Was it because markets fell?"). Ask "What was going on then?"
- Silence is fine. Count to 5 before the next question.
- Don't explain, correct or advise, even if they misunderstand returns. Note it; misunderstanding *is* data.
- Don't pitch features or ideas. Not even at the end.
- Use their words back to them ("You said it felt 'pointless'. Tell me more about that.").

Key questions have a Hinglish alternative in *italics*.

### 0. Warm-up (3 min)
- "Tell me a little about yourself: what you do, roughly where you are in life right now."
  *"Apne baare mein thoda batao, kya karte ho, life abhi kis stage pe hai?"*
- "How do you usually handle money each month: what comes in, what goes out first?"
  *"Har mahine paise ka hisaab kaise chalta hai? Salary aane ke baad pehle kya kya jaata hai?"*

Note: income rhythm (fixed date / irregular), dependants, shared expenses.

### 1. Why they started and what they expected (5 min) - *Brief area 1*
- "Take me back to when you started your first SIP. What was happening in your life then?"
  *"Jab pehli SIP shuru ki thi, us time life mein kya chal raha tha?"*
- "What made you actually do it that day, rather than some other day?"
  *"Usi din kyun kiya? Kya hua tha jo trigger bana?"*
- "Who or what got you thinking about it?" (friend, YouTube, office, parent)
- "How did you pick the fund and the amount?"
  *"Fund aur amount kaise decide kiya?"*
- "At that point, what did you expect would happen with it? Over what time?"
  *"Us time aapko kya lagta tha, isse kya hoga? Kitne time mein?"*

Probes: Was there a goal? Was it written down anywhere? Did they plan for how long? What did "doing well" mean to them then? Did they set up autopay/mandate themselves; what was that like?

### 2. SIP journey timeline exercise (8-10 min) - *Brief area 2*
Say: "Let's draw a quick timeline together. Start from the day you started, and walk me month by month to today. For each month, tell me anything you remember: instalments, checking the app, things in your life, things you heard. Mark the highs and the lows."
*"Chalo ek timeline banate hain. Jis din shuru kiya wahan se aaj tak, mahine-mahine batao kya hua. Ache moments aur bure moments dono."*

Interviewer sketches as they speak (template in Section 6):

```
Month:   M1 ---- M2 ---- M3 ---- ... ---- Today
Events:  started  first    ...
         autopay  debit
Feeling:  +  /  0  /  -
```

Probes for each marked point:
- "What happened there?" / "What happened next?"
- "How did you feel about the SIP at that point?"
- "Was anything else going on in life that month?" (job change, bills, festival, wedding, travel, salary delay)
- "Did the instalment go through that month? How did you know?"

At the end: "Looking at this whole line, which moment mattered most for whether you kept going?" -> carry that into Section 3.

### 3. Pausing, reducing, stopping: the drop-off deep-dive (8-10 min) - *Brief area 3*
Opening (ask everyone, including continuers):
- "Was there ever a time you missed an instalment, paused, reduced the amount, or thought about stopping?"
  *"Kabhi aisa hua ki instalment miss hui, pause kiya, amount kam kiya, ya band karne ka socha?"*

**If yes: critical-incident deep-dive on the most recent / most important moment.**
- "Take me to the week before that happened. What was going on?"
  *"Usse ek hafta pehle kya chal raha tha?"*
- "What was the first moment you thought about pausing/stopping? Where were you, what were you looking at?"
  *"Sabse pehli baar kab dimaag mein aaya ki band kar doon? Kahan the, kya dekh rahe the?"*
- "What were you thinking? What did you think would happen if you continued? If you stopped?"
- "Did you talk to anyone about it? What did they say?"
  *"Kisi se baat ki is baare mein? Unhone kya bola?"*
- "What did you look at before deciding: in the app, outside the app?"
- "How did you actually do it? Walk me through the steps." (pause button / cancel mandate / let it bounce / low balance / ignored it)
- "Was it a decision, or did it just sort of happen?"
  *"Yeh soch samajh ke kiya, ya bas ho gaya?"*
- "How did you feel right after? And now?"
- "Since then, have you restarted or thought about restarting? What happened?"
- "Looking back, was there anything at that point, from anyone or anywhere, that would have changed what you did?"
  *"Peeche dekh ke, us time kya cheez hoti toh aap continue karte?"*

Separate the layers in notes: **trigger** (the event) / **interpretation** (what they thought it meant) / **enabler** (what made stopping easy) / **missing support** (what wasn't there).

**If no (continuers):**
- "Was there a month that was harder than others? Tell me about it."
- "What kept it going that month?" (autopay inertia, a goal, a person, didn't look)
- "Has a friend or colleague of yours stopped? What happened with them?" (secondary evidence, mark as such)

**If they say "markets fell":** "What exactly did you see? Where? What number? What did you think that meant for *your* money? What else was happening that month? You'd started knowing markets go up and down; what made this time different?"

### 4. Checking behaviour (5 min) - *Brief area 4*
Remind: "Please don't open the app or share your screen; just describe from memory."
*"App mat kholo, bas yaad se batao."*
- "Tell me about the last time you opened the app to look at your SIP. When was it, and what made you open it?"
  *"Last time kab app khol ke SIP dekhi thi? Kyun kholi thi?"*
- "What screen did you land on? What's the first number you look at?"
  *"Kaunsi screen pe gaye? Sabse pehle kaunsa number dekhte ho?"*
- "What did that number say, roughly? What does that number mean to you?" (probe: total value, returns %, 1-day change, XIRR, invested vs current)
- "How did you feel when you saw it?"
  *"Woh dekh ke kaisa laga?"*
- "How often do you check? Has that changed since you started?"
- "Do notifications from the app bring you in? Which ones do you remember?"
- "Is there anything on that screen you find confusing or ignore?"

Watch for: checking frequency spikes before a drop-off; misreading short-term returns; comparing to FD/savings; looking at absolute loss instead of %.

### 5. Outside the app when unsure (4 min) - *Brief area 5*
- "Tell me about the last time you weren't sure about your SIP or your fund. What did you do?"
  *"Last time jab SIP ya fund ke baare mein doubt hua, tab kya kiya?"*
- "Where did you go: YouTube, a friend, family, Reddit, a CA, Google, anything?"
  *"Kahan dekha ya kisse poocha: YouTube, dost, ghar wale, Reddit, CA?"*
- "What did you search or ask, in your own words?"
- "What did they tell you / what did you find? What did you do after that?"
- "Whose opinion do you trust most on money? Why them?"
- "Has anything you saw outside the app made you feel better or worse about continuing?"

### 6. What would make them continue, framed around the past (4 min) - *Brief area 6*
Do NOT ask "What features would you want?" Anchor in real moments:
- "Think of the month you [paused / nearly stopped / found hardest]. What would have needed to be different for you to keep going?"
  *"Jis mahine aapne [pause kiya / band karne ka socha], us time kya alag hota toh aap continue karte?"*
- "Think of a time something in your life helped you stick to a habit or saving plan. What helped?"
  *"Kabhi koi saving ya habit jo aapne lambe time tak continue ki? Usme kya help hua?"*
- (Continuers) "What do you think has kept you going so far?"
- "If you've restarted, or thought about it, what got you there?"

Log answers as **stated wishes**, separate from observed behaviour (see Section 7).

### 7. Wrap-up (2 min)
- "Is there anything about your SIP experience I didn't ask about that you think matters?"
  *"Kuch aur hai jo maine nahi poocha par aapko lagta hai important hai?"*
- "Who else do you know who started a SIP recently, and might talk to me?" (especially stoppers)
- Thank them. Do not give advice even if asked; say "I'm not the right person for advice, but happy to share general resources after."
- Stop the recording. Spend 10 minutes filling the note sheet *immediately*.

**Timing summary**

| Section | Min |
|---|---|
| Consent | 2 |
| 0 Warm-up | 3 |
| 1 Why started / expectations | 5 |
| 2 Timeline | 8-10 |
| 3 Drop-off deep-dive | 8-10 |
| 4 Checking | 5 |
| 5 Outside the app | 4 |
| 6 What would help continue | 4 |
| 7 Wrap-up | 2 |
| **Total** | **~40** |

If running short on time, protect Sections 2 and 3; shorten 5 and 6. If adding a module (Section 5 below), cut warm-up and Section 1 to 3 min each and fold the module in after Section 3.

## 5. Optional probe modules

Pick 1-2 once the segment is chosen. Each adds ~5 min. Insert after Section 3.

### A. Irregular income / first salary
- "Walk me through what happened to money in a month when income came late or was lower than usual."
  *"Jis mahine paise late aaye ya kam aaye, tab kya kya hua?"*
- "When is your SIP date compared to when money comes in? How was that date chosen?"
- "In a tight month, what's the order things get paid in? Where does the SIP sit?"
- (First salary) "What else were you spending on in the first few months of earning? How did the SIP compare?"
- "Has the SIP ever bounced or been skipped because the balance was low? What happened after?"
- Listen for: SIP date mismatch, SIP seen as "optional" vs rent/EMI, no buffer, guilt vs relief after skipping.

### B. Returns comprehension
- "In your own words, what does the main number on your portfolio screen tell you?"
- "Tell me about a time the number surprised you. What did you think was happening?"
- "How do you decide whether your SIP is 'doing well' or 'doing badly'? Compared to what?"
  *"Kaise decide karte ho ki SIP achha chal raha hai ya bura? Kisse compare karte ho?"*
- "Have you ever compared it with an FD, savings account, or a friend's returns? What happened?"
- Listen for: confusing absolute vs %, XIRR vs absolute return, short-horizon judgement, expecting linear growth, red = "losing money".

### C. Mandate / autopay failure
- "Tell me how you set up the autopay/mandate. What was that like?"
  *"Autopay ya mandate kaise set kiya tha? Kaisa experience tha?"*
- "Has an instalment ever failed? How did you find out? What did the message say?"
- "What did you do next? How long did it take to fix, if at all?"
- "Did you ever change bank accounts or cards? What happened to the SIP?"
- Listen for: silent failures, bank vs app blame, mandate expiry or limits, failure read as a sign to stop, effort to restart.

### D. Influencer- / friend-led starters
- "Tell me about the video/post/person that got you to start. What did they say?"
- "What did you think would happen after you started, based on that?"
- "Since then, have you gone back to them, or to someone else? What did you hear?"
- "Has anything you heard later contradicted what got you started? What did you do?"
- Listen for: borrowed conviction (no personal goal), expectation gaps from content, fund-switching or stopping driven by new content, FOMO into other assets (stocks, F&O, crypto).

### E. Students / family-funded investors
- "Where does the money for your SIP come from? Who knows about it?"
  *"SIP ke paise kahan se aate hain? Ghar mein kisko pata hai?"*
- "Tell me about a time something came up (fees, trip, gadget) that competed with the SIP. What happened?"
- "Has anyone in your family said anything about it, good or bad?"
- Listen for: allowance volatility, permission/visibility, small amounts feeling pointless, life transitions (graduation, first job, moving cities).

Starter hypotheses to track (replace with the discovery engine's actual themes when ready):
- H1: SIP date does not match when money actually arrives, so instalments fail or feel unaffordable.
- H2: Autopay/mandate failures are silent or confusing, and a failure gets read as "maybe I should stop".

- H5: Pausing is easy and restarting is not, so a "temporary" pause becomes permanent.
- H6: Advice outside the app (YouTube, friends) pulls money towards other things (stocks, F&O, switching funds) rather than continuing.

## 7. Synthesis plan

### Step 1. Debrief each interview (same day, 15 min)
Complete the note sheet. Write the participant's story in 3 sentences: *started because... then... continuation broke / held because...*

### Step 2. Affinity mapping (after all interviews, ~2 hours)
1. Turn every observation and quote into one sticky (Miro/FigJam or paper). Tag each with participant code and type: **behaviour** / **feeling** / **belief** / **stated wish**.
2. Cluster bottom-up by similarity. Do not start from the discovery engine's themes; let groups form, then name them.
3. Name each cluster as an insight sentence, not a topic. Not "Autopay" but "A failed autopay felt like the app giving up, so they gave up too."
4. Lay clusters along the journey: start -> first months -> first wobble -> drop-off/hold -> after.
5. For each cluster, note which participants appear and whether they are continuers or stoppers. A theme that appears **only** in stoppers is a candidate root cause; one that appears in **both** is probably not the differentiator, so look for what continuers had that stoppers lacked.

### Step 3. Validate / challenge / new table (vs the AI discovery engine)

| Discovery engine theme | Volume in engine (n mentions) | Interview evidence (P#, quote/event) | Verdict | What interviews add (the "why") |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| e.g. T1: mandate failures | | P3, P5: "..." | Validated | Failure message read as rejection |
| e.g. T2: returns confusion | | P1 contradicts | Challenged | Confusion present but didn't drive stopping |
| (none) | - | P2, P4, P6 | **New** | e.g. SIP date set on signup day, not payday |

Verdict rules:
- **Validated:** at least 2 participants show it in *behaviour* (not just opinion), and it plausibly explains a drop-off.
- **Challenged:** participants experienced it but continued anyway, or the story contradicts the engine's interpretation.
- **New:** appears in at least 2 interviews and is absent or weak in the engine data. Flag that review data has a bias here (people rarely write reviews about quietly stopping).
- **Not tested:** the sample didn't cover it. Say so.

### Step 4. Root-cause statement
Write the problem as: *[Segment] who start a SIP intending to continue break off when [specific moment/trigger], because [mechanism], and nothing in [app / their environment] [helps them interpret / recover / adjust].* Check it is NOT "investors stop when markets fall". If market movement is involved, the problem statement must name the *mechanism* (e.g. misreading a short-term number with no goal to anchor to).

### Step 5. Showing it in the deck (2-3 slides)
1. **Who we spoke to:** participant grid (P1-P6: status, income type, months since start, platform, influence source). No names.
2. **Journey with drop-off moments:** one combined timeline showing where each participant wobbled or broke, with 1 short quote per key moment and the emotion curve.
3. **Validated / challenged / new table** (condensed) plus the root-cause statement and 1-2 strongest quotes.
State limitations in one line: "6 interviews, friends-and-colleagues network, urban 20-35; used to explain why, not how many."

### Pitfalls to avoid
- **Leading questions.** "Was it because the market fell?" plants the answer. Ask "What was going on then?"
- **Hypotheticals as evidence.** "Would you use a feature that...?" answers predict nothing. Ask about what happened.
- **Solutioning during interviews.** Do not pitch, test ideas or explain features. It turns the interview into a polite approval session.
- **Treating stated wishes as evidence.** "I'd continue if there were reminders" is a clue to investigate, not a finding. Only count it if their past behaviour supports it.
- **Correcting or advising.** If they misread returns, that misreading is the data. Don't fix it on the call.
- **Over-weighting one vivid story.** One dramatic quote is not a pattern. Require 2+ participants for a validated insight.
- **Confirmation bias against the engine.** Look actively for interviews that *contradict* the AI themes; challenged themes are valuable.
- **Friends being nice.** Discount generic praise of the app; focus on concrete events.
- **Collecting sensitive data.** If a participant starts reading out account numbers, PAN or offers a screenshot, stop them politely and don't record it. Amounts as ranges only.
- **Percentages from n=6.** Use "4 of 6", never "67%".
